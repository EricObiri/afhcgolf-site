## Alpha Achievers

Alpha Achievers is an educational program of the Kappa Phi Lambda Chapter of Howard County, Maryland, for African-American young men in grades 9–12. It fosters a positive learning environment where students pursue excellence by reaching, keeping and exceeding a 3.0 grade point average, the minimum for membership, and it encourages students below 3.0 to work toward that goal.

In existence since 1997, the program also builds character and leadership and encourages its members to become full citizens of their school and community. Membership runs to 250–400 students each school year across Howard County's twelve high schools, supported by 12 school-based teacher-advisors and 12 members of Alpha Phi Alpha. The young men deliver over 7,770 hours of community service each school year.

Success in school is the foundation for success in adult life. Alpha Achievers helps deter negative influences and gives young men a place to bond with others who share their academic goals.

## Harambee Conference

This annual half-day conference brings together student representatives of Alpha Achievers chapters across Howard County to learn and collaborate through guest speakers, workshops and a luncheon built around a theme.

## Leadership Development Institute

The Leadership Development Institute (LDI) develops a 21st-century generation of African-American leaders. Kappa Phi Lambda and other chapters in the region sponsor and run this annual three-day program of intensive leadership training for high-school-age young men, held at a local college or university. With professionals and experts, workshop leaders teach parliamentary procedure, conflict resolution, models of leadership, public speaking, community and civic engagement, and study skills.

## Project Alpha

[Project Alpha](https://apa1906.net/national-programs/project-alpha/) is one of the fraternity's national programs. It serves boys aged 12–15 with factual information about male roles in relationships, domestic violence, sexual behavior and attitudes, and helps them shape their futures and build a positive self-image.

## Scholarships and academic programs

### KPL scholarships for high school students

College scholarships that reward academic achievement and help with financial need among African-American students in Howard County public high schools. Since 1975 the foundation, with the Kappa Phi Lambda Chapter, has awarded over $350,000 in scholarships.

### Nu Kappa book scholarships

Two book scholarships for members of the Nu Kappa Chapter of Alpha Phi Alpha at UMBC.

### Edward D. Young Scholarship Award Ceremony

A ceremony honoring each year's KPL scholarship recipients.

### Ojise Oratorical Contest

Encourages critical thinking and analysis among African-American young men in the Howard County Public School System, and introduces them to research, history and literature of the African Diaspora. The top three essays receive financial awards.

### AP Success Grants

Grants for African-American young men in need who want to complete Advanced Placement courses or exams, or independent research projects. The program addresses low AP participation among African-American male students in Howard County.

### Advisor recognition

Support and recognition for Alpha Achievers school advisors, with information about upcoming events.

### College tour

A UMBC campus tour for 50 high-achieving students.

### Financial literacy

Sessions on credit for students and their parents.

### Patron ads

Program advertising that funds the scholarships.

## A Voteless People Is a Hopeless People

Alpha began this national program in the 1930s, when many African-Americans had the right to vote but were kept from voting by poll taxes, threats of reprisal and lack of information about the process. Voter education and registration has been its focus for nearly 100 years. Since the 1990s it has also covered political awareness and empowerment, most often through town meetings and candidate forums.
